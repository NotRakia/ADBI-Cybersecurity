# UNODC: Darknet Cybercrime Threats in SEA

## Overview

Cybercrime is an evolving form of transnational crime. The material explains the types of cyberspace, the development and operation of darknets and the Tor network, the use of anonymity technology and cryptocurrencies in illicit markets, and cybercrime-as-a-service (CaaS), including malware, ransomware, and denial-of-service attacks.

## Key Concepts

- Types of Cyberspace: Clearnet is the publicly accessible Internet; Deep Web contains content unknown to search engines; Darkweb consists of websites on darknets that often require special software to access.
- Darknet Development: Popular darknets include Freenet (2000), GNUnet (2001), Tor (2002), WASTE and I2P (2003), Onion services (2004), RetroShare (2006), and Silk Road (2011).
- Tor Network: Provides anonymity for Internet users and online services by routing traffic through volunteer relays and encrypting data multiple times. Onion services use `.onion` addresses and preserve the anonymity of both parties.
- Censorship Circumvention: The practice of bypassing Internet censorship techniques to access blocked information or services.
- Cryptocurrencies and Blockchain: Cryptocurrencies are virtual assets based on distributed networks. A blockchain is a public transaction record showing transactions between Bitcoin wallets.
- Crypto-Mixers: Services that mix identifiable cryptocurrency tokens with other funds to obscure the trail to the original source.
- Cybercrime-as-a-Service (CaaS): The trade of compromised financial information and the provision of cybercrime tools and services through illicit markets.
- Payment Card Fraud: The acquisition and unauthorized use of payment card data to purchase products.
- Malware-as-a-Service (MaaS): Malware software and toolkits provided as a service, enabling non-cyber specialists to infect systems and control them for illegal use.
- Cyberattack: The deliberate exploitation of computer systems and networks to take over or cause damage to a victim.
- Ransomware and Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS): Ransomware prevents victims from accessing their data and may threaten to publish it unless a ransom is paid. RaaS provides ransomware and its control systems to customers for criminal use.
- Denial-of-Service (DoS): An attack that disrupts a service's normal function and prevents others from accessing it. Criminals sell DoS attacks as a service on the Darkweb, often using botnets.

## Key Takeaways

1. Cybercrime is an evolving form of transnational crime, and darknet technologies have increased the accessibility of illicit marketplaces.
2. The Tor network provides anonymity by routing Internet traffic through volunteer relays, encrypting data multiple times, and concealing users' and servers' IP addresses.
3. Cryptocurrencies and crypto-mixers are used in darknet transactions, although blockchain records transactions between Bitcoin wallets.
4. Cybercrime-as-a-service enables criminals to obtain compromised financial information, malware, ransomware, and denial-of-service attacks as services.
5. Darknet-related threats include payment card fraud, unauthorized access, malware infections, ransomware extortion, and denial-of-service attacks.

## Key Application

1. Distinguish between the Clearnet, Deep Web, and Darkweb when assessing online environments and potential cybercrime threats.
2. Understand how Tor, onion services, and encryption provide anonymity and complicate the identification of users and servers.
3. Monitor and protect payment card information and financial data against unauthorized use and exposure.
4. Strengthen defenses against malware, ransomware, and DoS attacks through security controls, system monitoring, and incident response.
5. Recognize how cybercrime-as-a-service allows individuals without specialized cybersecurity expertise to obtain tools and services for conducting cyberattacks.
