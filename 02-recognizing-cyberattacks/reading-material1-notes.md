## Crowdstrike 2026 Global Threat Report
## Overview

Interactive intrusions continued to expand as adversaries increasingly favored direct human-driven attacks over traditional malware-based attacks. Threat actors use legitimate credentials, native tools, and administrative functions to move laterally and achieve objectives while blending into normal user behavior. AI enhances established tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), while supply chain attacks exploit trust in legitimate software, third-party dependencies, and SaaS providers.
## Key Concept

- Interactive Intrusions: Threat actors engage directly with victim environments, using legitimate credentials, native tools, and administrative functions to move laterally and achieve objectives while blending into normal user behavior.
- Break Out and Breakout Time: Break Out is the term for the objective of the adversaries once they gain initial access and move laterally from the initial foothold to high-value assets. Breakout Time is the term for the speed of break out.
- Threats From AI: Threat actors use AI to enhance social engineering operations’ credibility and scale, generate convincing content and personas, accelerate malware development, generate code, create exploits, and generate commands for further exploitation.
- Supply Chain Attacks: Threat actors modify software provider infrastructure or code bases, compromise software providers, manipulate update mechanisms, or obtain credentials and modify legitimate software packages in public code repositories.
- Third-Party Software Dependency Compromise: Adversaries abuse trust in third-party packages to introduce malicious code into otherwise legitimate projects. Organizations then unknowingly execute untrusted code after installing or updating software that includes these dependencies.
- SaaS Providers and Zero-Day Exploits: Attackers can steal SaaS authentication tokens from a centralized repository and use them to access many customer data stores. Zero-day exploits allow adversaries to evade detection and countermeasures for identified exploit vectors.
- Emerging Threat Landscape: Threat actors will continue adopting AI for social engineering, IO, and technical activity. The ransomware ecosystem remains resilient, while fast-paced vishing campaigns targeting SaaS applications for initial access and data exfiltration will almost certainly continue.

## Key Takeaways

1. Interactive intrusions increasingly rely on legitimate credentials, native tools, and administrative functions, making malicious activity difficult to distinguish from authorized activity.
2. The technology sector remained the most frequently targeted industry, while consulting, manufacturing, retail, and other data-rich industries also experienced elevated activity.
3. AI primarily enhances established tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), accelerating social engineering, malware development, and post-exploitation activities.
4. Supply chain attacks exploit trust in legitimate software, third-party dependencies, and SaaS providers to deploy malicious code, leak credentials, exfiltrate sensitive data, and compromise external systems.
5. Organizations must strengthen and adapt their defenses against increasingly sophisticated threats, including AI-enabled attacks, supply chain compromises, ransomware, and vishing campaigns targeting SaaS applications.

## Key Application

1. Monitor legitimate credentials, native tools, administrative functions, and lateral movement to identify malicious intent within otherwise legitimate actions.
2. Prioritize incident response based on Breakout Time to reduce the costs and damages associated with an intrusion.
3. Assess AI-related risks involving AI models, training data, agents, and supply chains as organizations embed AI into core business processes.
4. Strengthen software supply chain security by verifying third-party dependencies, software updates, provider infrastructure, and authentication credentials.
5. Adapt security defenses to detect and respond to AI-enhanced social engineering, SaaS-focused vishing, ransomware, and data exfiltration.
