## Overview

The VERIS framework (Vocabulary for Event Recording and Incident Sharing) provides a consistent, unequivocal collection of security incident details. It classifies incidents based on threat actors, threat actions, assets, and attributes (the 4As) to understand who is responsible, how an incident occurred, what was affected, and what impact it had.

Cybersecurity threats continue to evolve, with increasing zero-day and critical vulnerabilities, GenAI-augmented malware, complex social engineering, ransomware, and third-party risks. Exploitation of vulnerabilities is now the most common initial access vector for breaches, while financial gain remains the leading driver for cybercriminals. Human errors, stolen credentials, insider misuse, and denial-of-service attacks remain significant threats across industries.

## Key Concepts

- VERIS Framework: A framework designed to have a consistent, unequivocal collection of security incident details.
- 4As: Actors (who), Action (how), Asset (what), and Attribute (so what).
- Threat Actors: External actors, internal actors, partners, or multiple combinations responsible for an incident.
- Threat Actions: Seven primary categories: Malware, Hacking, Social, Misuse, Physical, Error, and Environmental.
- Assets: Targets of attacks, including servers, persons, user devices, networks, and media.
- Attributes: The CIA triad—Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability—describes the effects of an incident on an asset.
- Variety: More specific enumerations of higher-level categories, such as SQL injection, brute force, or organized criminal groups.
- Incident vs. Breach: An incident compromises the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an information asset. A breach results in the confirmed disclosure of data to an unauthorized party.
- Third-Party Incidents: Classified into three types: vendors in an organization's software supply chain, vendors hosting organizational data, and vendors connected to the organization's environment.
- Vulnerability Management: A risk mitigation process that addresses vulnerabilities, prioritizes patching, and reduces opportunities for exploitation.
- GenAI in Cybersecurity: Threat actors use GenAI for targeting, initial access, malware development, and other attack tools. Source code is the most common type submitted to external GenAI models.
- Social Engineering: Attackers use deception, manipulation, intimidation, phishing, and pretexting to exploit people and gain unauthorized access.
- Incident Classification Patterns: System Intrusion, Social Engineering, Basic Web Application Attacks, Miscellaneous Errors, Privilege Misuse, and Denial of Service.
- Threat Intelligence: Recorded observations of techniques used by threat actors to understand threats and inform defensive measures.
- Red Team Activity: Ethical hackers test an organization's ability to detect and respond to attacks, including through assume-compromise exercises.
- Industry Risks: Education, financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, public administration, and retail face threats involving ransomware, vulnerability exploitation, stolen credentials, phishing, human error, and third-party exposure.
- Small and Medium-Sized Businesses: Disproportionately impacted by ransomware and exposed to many of the same threats as larger organizations.
- AI Crawlers and Fetchers: AI crawlers gather data for model training and fine-tuning, while AI fetchers retrieve information in response to user requests or AI-agent tasks.

## Key  Takeaways
- VERIS framework provides a consistent way to record, classify, and analyze security incidents using the 4As: Actors, Action, Asset, and Attribute.
- Top cybersecurity threats persist like Exploitation of vulnerabilities, ransomware, stolen credentials, phishing, and social engineering.
- Third-party relationships increase an organization's exposure to security incidents through software supply chains, hosted data, and connected environments.
- Vulnerability management, access controls, monitoring, and timely patching are important risk mitigation measures.
- Human errors, misconfigurations, misdelivery, and insider misuse can cause data breaches and require appropriate safeguards.
- GenAI can assist threat actors in targeting, gaining initial access, and developing malware and other attack tools.
- Financial gain remains the leading driver for cybercriminals, making ransomware and data theft major concerns across industries.

## Key Application
- Incident Classification: Apply the VERIS 4As to document and classify security incidents consistently.
- Risk Assessment: Identify vulnerable assets, threat actors, attack methods, and potential impacts to prioritize security risks.
- Vulnerability Management: Prioritize critical vulnerabilities and apply patches to reduce opportunities for exploitation.
- Incident Response: Use incident details and threat intelligence to investigate attacks, identify entry points, and improve detection and response.
- Access Control: Enforce appropriate permissions, monitor privileged accounts, and protect against stolen credentials and insider misuse.
