# Commonwealth Tackling Rising Cybercrime Threat in Asia

## Overview

Cybercrime is an increasing threat in Asia, it causes threats to intellectual property and security, financial losses, psychological stress and disruption to services and assets growing the issues for governments and businesses.

A conference was held by Commonwealth Secretariat, and they include law enforcement officials, prosecutors, judges and magistrates, key domestic policy makers, international security experts, and academics to find solutions to the growing cybersecurity issues of the region.

The conference was attended by international experts from INTERPOL, Meta, the US Department of Justice, the Singapore Ministry of Home Affairs, the Singapore Police Force, Cyber Security Agency Singapore, legal practitioners and academics. Delegates shared views on the kinds, trends, and effects of cybercrime, importance of cyber diplomacy, mutual legal assistance, and practical solutions to securing electronic evidence across territorial borders.

## Key Concepts

### Cybercrime Threats and Impact

- Cybercrime is a threat to countries' strategic interests, like trade and investment.
- Cybercrime risks the key economic sectors, critical national infrastructure, defence and security interests,
- democratic institutions and systems, health, safety and well-being of each and
- every one of us, and children the most vulnerable in society.
- Cybercrime risks the impact on intellectual property rights regimes, critical national infrastructure, and great disruptions to businesses and key services sectors which COVID-19 ampliefied.

### Cybercrime Statistics in Asia

- Asia region is mostly targeted by ransomware attacks.
- Asian Pacific region experienced a 168% year-on-year increase in cyberattacks in May 2021 compared to May 2020.
- Top Asia-Pacific Region to suffer largest increase in Cyberattacks were Japan, Singapore, Indonesia, and Malaysia.
- Scams in Singapore make up more than half of the reported cases of crimes.

### Top Cyber threats in Asia

- **Ransomware**
- **Scams**
- **Crime as a Service Attacks** is the practice of cybercriminals selling access to the tools and knowledge needed to execute a cybercrime.
- **Hacking and Denial of Service Attacks** are attacks designed to crash IT systems.

### Economic Impact and Cybersecurity Spending

- Global cybercrime are expected to rise 15% annually and reach USD 10.5 trillion by 2025, higher than market for illicit drugs.
- Asia-Pacific region is likely to spend over USD 31 Billion on cybersecurity solutions and services by 2022 15.5% increase than 2021.

### Challenges in Law Enforcement

- Anonymity gained by tools like VPN, dark web, and crypto currencies gives great challenges to law enforcement officials in identifying and the prosecution of cybercriminals.

## Key Takewayas

- Cybercrime is an increasing threat in Asia, affecting intellectual property, security, businesses, and individuals.
- Cybercrime is a threat to countries' strategic interests, including trade and investment.
- Asia region is mostly targeted by ransomware attacks.
- Cybercrime poses challenges to law enforcement officials in identifying and the prosecution of cybercriminals.
- International cooperation and practical solutions are needed to address the growing cybersecurity issues of the region.

## Practical Application

- Understand the kinds, trends, and effects of cybercrime.
- Recognize ransomware, scams, Crime as a Service Attacks, hacking and Denial of Service Attacks.
- Understand the importance of cyber diplomacy and mutual legal assistance.
- Learn practical solutions to securing electronic evidence across territorial borders.
- Recognize the importance of cybersecurity solutions and services in protecting businesses, key services sectors, and critical national infrastructure.
